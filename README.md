## What Is This?

The Ministry of the Allied is a World of Warcraft: Forever guild built around a fully functioning political system. Members are citizens. The guild has a government. Laws are passed, elections are held, and power changes hands.

This is not a roleplay guild in the traditional sense. You do not need to speak in character or commit to a persona. The political structure is the game layer we have built on top of WoW — and it is designed to make the social experience of the guild genuinely more interesting than anything the game provides on its own.

If you have ever wanted to run for office, start a political party, lead a campaign, expose a corrupt government, or just vote in an election that actually means something — this is the guild for you.

## How It Works

### The Crown
The guild is governed under a constitutional monarchy. Two permanent co-monarchs hold the Crown and represent the continuity and identity of the Ministry. The Crown does not stand for election. It endures.

The Crown holds reserved powers — including the ability to dissolve a government — but exercises them at political cost. The monarchy is not above consequence.

### The Government
Executive power sits with an elected **Prime Minister**, who appoints their own **Cabinet** of ministers. The Prime Minister leads the government for a term of three months, sets policy, manages the treasury, and directs the guild's activities.

### The Opposition
The losing party in every election forms the **Official Opposition**, holding the government to account between elections and presenting an alternative programme for the next vote.

### Elections
A **General Election** is held every three months. Two registered parties field candidates. All citizens vote directly. The winner takes office. A formal **debate** is held in the campaign period — open to all citizens to observe and question.

### Laws
Governments pass laws that govern guild life — tax rates, loot rules, recruitment criteria, trade conduct. All laws last exactly **three months** and must be actively renewed by incoming governments. Nothing is permanent. Every election produces a genuine reconsideration of policy.

### No Confidence
Citizens can bring down a government between elections through a **Vote of No Confidence**. Votes are submitted by in-game mail to the Crown. If 40% of the active citizen population submits within seven days, the government falls and a Snap Election is called.

### The Districts
Citizens are assigned to one of **five regional districts**, each anchored to a location in Stormwind. Each district elects a **Regional Representative** to the advisory council. Districts have their own character and political identity.

| District | Location | Character |
|---|---|---|
| The Old Quarter | Old Town | Military, PvP, defence |
| The Arcane Ward | Mage Quarter | Crafting, intellect, strategy |
| The Iron District | Dwarven District | Industry, mining, production |
| The Cathedral Ward | Cathedral Square | Justice, ceremony, diplomacy |
| The Harbour Quarter | Stormwind Harbour | Trade, commerce, external affairs |

### Justice
An independent **High Judiciary** of three Crown-appointed Justices handles accusations of law-breaking. The judiciary is separate from the government and cannot be directed by the Prime Minister. Punishments range from public censure and treasury fines to trade embargo and exile.

## The Two Parties

### The Silverhand
*Collective strength. Shared prosperity. The light protects all.*

The Silverhand believes the Ministry is only as strong as its weakest citizen. They stand for funded commons, progressive taxation, needs-based loot distribution, and support for new members. Named after the Order of the Silver Hand — those who gave everything in service of something greater than themselves.

### The Azeroth Exchange
*Free citizens. Open markets. Rise on your own terms.*

The Azeroth Exchange believes the Ministry thrives when its citizens are free to thrive. They stand for low taxation, a free guild market, merit-based raiding, and lean government. Named after the principle that drives every great market: free exchange between free agents.

## Who Should Join

- Players who want a guild with real community depth beyond just raiding
- People who enjoy politics, debate, and social dynamics
- Anyone who wants their decisions to actually matter

- Raiders, crafters, PvPers, traders — all play styles have a home and a political voice
